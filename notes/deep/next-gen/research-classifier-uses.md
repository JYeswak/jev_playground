# Fast classifiers and judges in computer use and games, 2025–2026

The ask: find published work where a small, fast judge is the part that makes the system work. Every number below comes from the abstract, the paper's HTML or a README, and I read each one this session unless the row says otherwise. Rows marked "scan" come from `outside-scan-20260924.md`. Arithmetic and judgment of mine are marked [INFERENCE]. I made no edits and no model calls.

Each row ends with **Jev:** a line on what Jev's shape changes. Jev asks typed questions (Choice / Score / Noul) about a JSON state, takes about 130 ms, costs about $0.02 per 1,000 answers, flips 0–2 answers between identical runs, and reads text only.

## 1. Computer use, GUI and web agents

**Step verifiers, critics and reward models**
- **Web-Shepherd**, May 2025, [2505.15277](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15277). A step-level reward model trained on 40K preference pairs. About 30 points more accurate than GPT-4o on WebRewardBench. As the verifier for a GPT-4o-mini policy on WebArena-lite it scored 10.9 points higher, at 10× less cost than GPT-4o-mini as verifier. **Jev:** it fills the same verifier slot with no training, and its checklist items map one-to-one onto parallel Nouls.
- **Online-Mind2Web judge**, Apr 2025, [2504.01382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01382). An LLM-as-judge for task success, about 85% agreement with humans. **AgentRewardBench**, Apr 2025, [2504.08942](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08942): 1,302 trajectories rated by experts; of 12 LLM judges none excels on every benchmark, and rule-based checkers under-report success. **Jev:** success, side effects and repetition become three Nouls in one call.
- **ExeVRM**, Mar 2026, [2603.10178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10178). An 8B model that judges success from execution video: 84.7% accuracy and 87.7% recall, beating GPT-5.2 and Gemini-3 Pro. **Jev:** it cannot read video. The equivalent input is AX-tree or DOM diffs.
- **HiViG**, Jun 2026, [2606.11078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11078). A critic that checks each action before it runs, using history and visual grounding. Success is +5.8% (Qwen3-VL-32B) and +9.0% (Gemini-3-Flash) over the strongest critic baseline. GUI-Critic-R1, Jun 2025, [2506.04614](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04614), is the earlier critic of this kind. **Jev:** a Noul "is this action correct?" per proposal; ~130 ms fits inside one step.
- **Autonomous evaluators**, Apr 2024 (foundational), [2404.06474](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06474). 74.4–92.9% agreement with oracle metrics; used to guide agents, they gave +29% on WebArena.

**Test-time search and candidate selection with a judge**
- **Agent S3, Behavior Best-of-N**, Oct 2025, [2510.02250](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02250). Runs several whole attempts and a judge picks the best one from its written summary. 69.9% on OSWorld (+7.3 absolute) with GPT-5; 60.2% (+10.4) with GPT-5 Mini; the human baseline is about 72%. **Jev:** picking one of N attempts is a Choice. Cheap per-step judging could move the selection from whole attempts to single steps.
- **GTA1**, Jul 2025, [2507.05791](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.05791). Samples several proposals per step and a judge selects one. 45.2% on OSWorld in 100 steps, against 42.9% for CUA o3 in 200 steps. **Jev:** a Choice over up to 255 proposals, in one call per step.
- **Agent Alpha**, Feb 2026, [2602.02995](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02995). Step-level MCTS that evaluates by comparison "to mitigate absolute scoring biases". About 77% on OSWorld. Koh et al., Jul 2024, [2407.01476](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01476): 26.4% on VisualWebArena (+39.7% relative). Agent Q, Aug 2024, [2408.07199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07199): 18.6% → 81.7%, and 95.4% with online search. **Jev:** Choice gives the policy prior, a pairwise Choice gives the value. typesafe-chess (scan) found absolute Scores saturate, which agrees with this.
- **WebDreamer**, Nov 2024 / Apr 2025, [2411.06559](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06559). An LLM simulates each action's outcome and scores it. Competitive with tree search at 4–5× less cost. **Jev:** it can score the simulated outcome but cannot write the simulation.

**Affordance and element scoring**
- **MindAct**, Jun 2023 (foundational), [2306.06070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06070). A DeBERTa ranker shortlists page elements for an LLM, with 85–89% recall in its top 50 ([Laya card](https://huggingface.co/Quantum08/laya-browser-mind2web)). **Laya-browser** (HF card, undated; checked 2026-09-24) is a 421M model with Jev-style typed heads, trained on Mind2Web. Its element accuracy is 50.3 / 48.2 / 48.4 on the cross-task / cross-website / cross-domain splits, against MindAct's 55.1 / 42.0 / 42.1, and its lexical shortlist loses 21–27% of steps. **Prune4Web**, Nov 2025, [2511.21398](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21398): 25–50× fewer candidates; GPT-4o-mini goes from 26.3% to 31.6%. **Jev:** Choice over up to 255 elements; the shortlist, not the chooser, sets the ceiling.
- **OmniParser V2**, Feb 2025, [MSR](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/articles/omniparser-v2-turning-any-llm-into-a-computer-use-agent/). A YOLO detector plus a caption model; 39.6 on ScreenSpot-Pro; 0.6 s per frame on an A100 and 0.8 s on an RTX 4090. **Jev:** it needs this kind of parser or an AX tree as its input.

**Speculation**
- **Speculative Actions**, Oct 2025, [2510.04371](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04371). A faster model guesses the next action: up to 55% next-action accuracy, up to 20% less latency. The paper calls for "selective branch launching". **AOSpec**, Aug 2026, [2608.00881](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00881): 11.8–32.5% lower mean latency on Terminal-Bench. **Jev:** it can predict which option the big model will choose, and its probability says whether a branch is worth launching.

**Monitors, guards and conformal methods**
- **Step-level cascade**, Apr 2026, [2604.27151](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27151). A ModernBERT (149M) was trained on GPT-5.2 labels from 300 trajectories. Its stuck detector scores 93.9% accuracy / 91.5 F1; its milestone detector 94.1% / 62.0 F1. With it, Qwen3-VL-8B plus Kimi K2.5 reaches 59.3% on OSWorld against 60.1% for Kimi alone. EvoCUA-8B plus Kimi reaches 58.2% at $0.051 per task (61.4% cheaper). **Jev:** these are text classifiers over the trajectory, which is exactly Jev's input, and Jev needs no per-agent training set.
- **CURA**, Aug 2026, [2608.27808](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27808). 64 of 71 failures ended with the agent claiming success. Its CUSUM alarm, a running statistical test with a false-alarm guarantee, catches 42.3% of failures a median of 31 steps early, at a realized false-alarm rate of 0.066. It forbids extra model calls. Its composite AUROC of 0.828 beats a total-token baseline by only +0.026 (p = 0.101). **Jev:** a calibrated "is it really done?" Noul could feed that running test.
- **Local CUA scaling**, Jul 2026, [2607.28573](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28573). As local agents get more context, their failures shift toward "premature false successes". **Automata from traces**, Aug 2026, [2608.23670](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23670): failure prediction reaches held-out AUROC 0.94.
- **DeAction**, Feb 2026, [2602.08995](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08995): F1 more than 15 points above baselines on MisActBench; attack success down by more than 90%. **SafePred**, Feb 2026, [2602.01725](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01725): over 97.6% safety, up to +21.4% task utility.
- **CORA**, Apr 2026, [2604.09155](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09155). Conformal risk control over a trained Guardian model's execute-or-abstain decision; no headline number in the abstract. **Argus**, Jun 2026, [2606.25760](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25760): conformal click regions lose coverage when the interface changes; rankings of uncertainty methods transfer poorly across model tiers (+0.08). **Jev:** conformal methods are already in GUI work (§3), but they gate or alarm; none produces a guaranteed set of candidate elements.
- **MacArena**, Jun 2026, [2606.06560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06560). 421 macOS tasks running in Apple Virtualization on Apple Silicon, with an AX tree in the observation. Model rankings invert between ported tasks and macOS-native ones (the leading model trails by more than 26% on the macOS subset). **Jev:** a local, AX-tree environment that runs on this Mac.
- **Ecosystem** (scan): jev-ultrafast cut browser protocol calls from 1,092 to 101 with 3/3 runs passing. The macos-computer-use-kit repo ships an unmeasured Jev guard before irreversible steps.

## 2. Games and embodied agents

**Latency decides**
- **VideoGameBench**, May 2025, [2505.18134](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18134). The best models complete 0.48% of the benchmark, and 1.6% of the Lite version, where the game pauses while the model thinks; the authors name latency as a major limit.
- **AgileThinker / Real-Time Reasoning Gym**, Nov 2025 (ICLR 2026), [2511.04898](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04898). If the agent is late, the environment applies a default action. Wall-clock scores (reactive / planning / AgileThinker): Freeway 0.24 / 0.12 / 0.88, Snake 0.37 / 0.04 / 0.45, Overcooked 0.57 / 0.00 / 0.89. **Jev:** it can be the reactive thread, returning a Choice with a probability.
- **Latent Bridge**, Jun 2026, [2606.24470](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24470). The reasoning VLM takes about 1.5 s per answer, too slow for a 15 Hz loop. A trained channel between slow and fast models gives MsPacman +57% and RoadRunner +28%; using both channels at once drops RoadRunner by 96%.
- **FPX**, May 2025, [2505.19481](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19481). Chooses model size per decision in Street Fighter; win rate up to +80%.
- **Lumine**, Nov 2025, [2511.08892](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08892): 5 Hz input, 30 Hz actions, reasoning "only when necessary". **Game-TARS**, Oct 2025, [2510.23691](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23691): "Sparse-Thinking"; about 2× the previous best success rate on Minecraft. **DPT-Agent**, Feb 2025, [2502.11882](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11882): a state machine as System 1 and an LLM as System 2.

**Policy and value priors in search**
- **MAV**, Dec 2024, [2412.12119](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12119): an LM guides MCTS and reaches grandmaster-level chess. **ChessBench**, Feb 2024 (foundational), [2402.04494](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04494): a 270M transformer reaches Lichess blitz Elo 2895 with no search. **PriorZero**, May 2026, [2605.12289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12289): the LLM prior is used only at the MCTS root. **typesafe-chess** (scan): with MCTS it loses 25 cp per move, against 168–190 for a single call.
- **NanoJev**, Sep 2026, [README](https://github.com/TianyuCodings/NanoJev) (author claim): ViZDoom Basic 128/128 against Jev's 56/128; Maze 4/10 against **Jev's 7/10**. **Jev:** it is weak on precise spatial aiming and competitive on navigation.

**Learned rewards, progress and interest**
- **Motif**, Sep 2023 (foundational), [2310.00166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00166). An LLM picks which of two text captions shows more progress; trained on that intrinsic reward alone, the NetHack agent outscores one trained on the game score. **ONI**, Oct 2024, [2410.23022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23022): asks the LLM asynchronously and distills the answers into a reward model, because asking at every observation does not scale.
- **VLM-AR3L**, Jul 2026, [2607.00483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00483): absolute plus relative VLM rewards; it reports that MineCLIP's rewards are unreliable. A triplet-loss study, Dec 2025, [2512.20675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.20675), found a simple loss beats more complex objectives. **R²VLM**, Mar 2026, [2603.17312](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17312): new state of the art on progress estimation.
- **Intelligent Go-Explore**, May 2024 (ICLR 2025), [2405.15143](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15143): a foundation model decides which archived states are interesting enough to return to. **OMNI-EPIC**, [2405.15568](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15568): VLMs were "not yet accurate enough" as success detectors, so the authors used code instead.
- **Text state beats pixels:** in BALROG, Nov 2024, [2411.13543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13543), several models did worse when given images. In "See, Symbolize, Act", Mar 2026, [2603.11601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11601), accurate symbolic state helped every model. OCAtari, [2306.08649](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08649), extracts object lists from Atari RAM. **Jev:** this is where a text-only judge fits games.
- **PokeAgent**, Mar 2026, [2603.15563](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15563): "considerable gaps" between LLM agents, specialist RL and elite humans.

**QA, content quality, integrity and NPCs**
- **TempGlitch**, May 2026, [2605.21443](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21443): 12 VLMs stay near chance on temporal glitches, and denser frame sampling or larger models do not fix it. **VideoGlitchBench**, Apr 2026, [2604.07818](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07818): 5,238 videos from 120 games.
- **TITAN**, Sep 2025, [2509.22170](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22170): LLM oracles over abstracted game state; 95% task completion, 4 previously unknown bugs, deployed in 8 QA pipelines.
- **PlaytestArena / Play2Code**, May 2026, [2605.28258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28258): generated games are graded against rubrics of expected in-play behaviour; 66.8% rubric pass rate (+37.1 over single-pass generation).
- **AntiCheatPT**, Aug 2025, [2508.06348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06348): 89.17% accuracy, AUC 93.36% on Counter-Strike 2 tick windows. **Turing Test on Screen**, Feb 2026, [2604.09574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09574): LMM agents are easily detected from their movement. **Jev:** both depend on numeric time series, and this repo's claim checks on numbers failed (R83), so these are a poor fit.
- **CPDC 2025 NPC challenge**, [2511.01720](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.01720): specialists built from LoRA adapters, ranked second; the paper cites a 7-second response limit.

## 3. Cross-cutting patterns

- **Distilling an expensive judge into a small one:** GPT-5.2 labels → ModernBERT (step-level cascade); LLM → reward model (ONI); Stockfish → a 270M transformer (ChessBench); models with Jev-style typed heads (NanoJev, Laya). **Jev:** zero-shot, with no distillation step.
- **A classifier labels the data:** **NitroGen**, Dec 2025, [2601.02427](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02427), reads controller overlays in gameplay video to recover the inputs (0.96 button accuracy, R² 0.84 for joysticks) over 40K hours, and gains up to 52% relative on unseen games. OS-Genesis, Dec 2024, [2412.19723](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19723), filters synthetic trajectories with a trajectory reward model.
- **Calibrated or conformal selection:** KnowNo (scan, 2023), CORA, CURA, role-stratified risk control for tool calls (Jul 2026, [2607.24343](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24343)).
- **Routers that decide when to call the big model:** the step-level cascade, TACIT-Switch (Aug 2026, [2608.27911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27911): +7.4–11.1 points in simulation; ALFWorld 48.5%), FPX, Lumine.

## 4. Gaps nobody has filled

1. **Let the reflex's confidence decide when to wait for the planner.** AgileThinker runs fixed threads, Lumine and Game-TARS learn when to think, DPT-Agent uses a state machine, and Latent Bridge trains a channel. None of them, as far as their abstracts say, lets a calibrated probability from the fast layer trigger the wait [INFERENCE]. Jev (~130 ms) could take RAM object lists from OCAtari and play Real-Time Reasoning Gym. The bar is AgileThinker's 0.88 / 0.45 / 0.89. Risk: NanoJev's 56/128 shows fine aiming is weak.
2. **Step monitors with no training set, feeding a certified alarm.** The cascade needed 300 labelled trajectories. CURA uses no model and barely beats a token count (p = 0.101), while 90% of its failures claimed success. Nouls for "stuck", "is the done-claim true" and "milestone reached" could feed CURA's running test (CUSUM) directly, at about $0.00002 per step [INFERENCE]. The ground truth is the OSWorld or MacArena task checkers.
3. **A conformal shortlist of elements instead of a fixed top-k.** MindAct gets 85–89% recall in its top 50, Laya loses 21–27% of steps, and Prune4Web has no guarantee. CORA and CURA use conformal methods only to gate or alarm, and Argus shows guarantees defined on screen pixels break when the interface changes. A KnowNo-style set built from Choice probabilities over AX-tree element IDs would carry a stated coverage and hand only that set to the big model. Risk: Jev put zero probability on the true answer in 5.9% of 77-option cases, so the guarantee must be calibrated per app.
4. **Calibrated speculation.** Speculative Actions reaches 55% accuracy and asks for selective branch launching. A Jev Choice predicting the big model's next action gives a probability to launch on; none of the speculation work uses a calibrated judge for this [INFERENCE].
5. **Relative progress judged online from text state, with no distillation step.** ONI built a distillation pipeline because asking at every observation was too expensive. Agent Alpha and VLM-AR3L find comparison beats absolute scoring. At about 80 answers/s, direct pairwise judging is feasible for about 10⁵–10⁶ steps (1M steps is about 3.5 hours) [INFERENCE]; that suits NetHack text and Pokémon speedrunning from RAM, not Atari-scale RL.
6. **Game QA oracles over state instead of pixels.** VLMs are near chance on temporal glitches (TempGlitch) and were judged unfit as success detectors (OMNI-EPIC), while rubric-based playtesting works (PlaytestArena). Glitch and rubric checks could run as parallel Nouls over engine or RAM state changes, fed into a fitted head; in this repo that approach reached AUROC 0.987 against 0.685 for a single verdict. Bugs injected with HackAtari would supply outside ground truth.
7. **Report judge stability.** AgentRewardBench found no dependable judge, and MacArena found rankings that invert. The judge papers I read report agreement, not how many answers flip between identical runs [INFERENCE, from abstracts]. Jev flips 0–2 answers where LLMs flip 9–97, so it could serve as a deterministic regression oracle, memoized by state hash, for A/B tests of harness changes.
