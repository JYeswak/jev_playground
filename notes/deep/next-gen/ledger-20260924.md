## 3. The regime pattern

**Where Jev wins.** It wins where the cheap lexical baseline collapses:
- BM25 scores 0.022 on NevIR negation.
- The keyword baseline scores 0.6329 on injection.
- TF-IDF falls to 0.70–0.73 on out-of-distribution spam while Jev holds 0.91–0.97.
- BM25's shortlist order is beaten by +0.086 nDCG.

Those sets had enough positives: injection 39.73%, phishing 50%, and a pre-filtered top-20 for rerank. Against LLM incumbents, Jev usually ties on raw accuracy. It wins on calibration (Brier, ECE), on stability (flips 0–2 vs 9–53), on cost (33–88×) and on large label sets, where Haiku's structured output fails 993 times out of 993.

**Where it loses.** It loses where the label lives in the tokens: phishing regex, grep-corpus lexical order, keyword triage, the harm rule. It loses where a constant dominates (always-bash), and where prevalence is low (9% toxicity; ~0.016% vetoes), because false positives swamp the rare true cases. It also loses when the question leaves the distribution it was framed on: the news persona applied to tool output, gate harm shapes it never saw, predicting which tool results a later turn needs (compaction), and checking numbers.

NE:3308 names a meta-gap. For 41 candidates, only floor comparisons were run, with "zero measurements" against an LLM arm.

## 6. Open doors

1. **A probe-vector feature factory.** Ask many cheap Noul probes per item and fit a head on them.
   - Evidence: 5 signals plus a logistic fit gave AUROC 0.9872 vs 0.6846 for the single verdict (R/oracle-kit-select-report-20260923.md:26-33). Signals plus fitting reached 95.1% vs regex 91.6% (NE:606-632). The probes cost $0.016–0.029 per 1,000.
   - Untested: prediction of real outcomes (Demand forecasting, Risk assessment); the autoresearch cookbook has never been run.
2. **A deterministic judge as a regression oracle.** Use Jev to diff agent or model behaviour in CI, and memoize judgments by state hash.
   - Evidence: flips of 0–1 vs 43–53; token counts deterministic; the score register already stores a sha256 per state (work/omp-jev-rerank/src/index.ts:26-33).
   - Untested.
3. **Auto-handle the confident bulk, send the tail to a human.**
   - Evidence: a 0.7 gate auto-routes 93% at 99.2% (R/choice-banking77-20260924.md:135-142). At confidence ≥0.99, 45% route at 97.0%. Abstaining won on CLINC. The cascade receipt says to escalate to a human, not to Haiku.
   - Untested on a live queue (claims, alerts, tickets).
4. **A cheap filter, then Jev, to raise prevalence.** Jev wins on shortlists and on sets with 40–50% positives; it loses at 9% and 0.016%.
   - Untested: an end-to-end two-stage pipeline on a low-prevalence stream.
5. **Corpus-scale map-reduce.** Throughput is about 80 calls/s with 0 failures across 18,000 calls. A million Noul answers would cost about $28.7 at 683 tokens each [INFERENCE].
   - Untested beyond 18,000 calls. Example targets: every agent turn, or every commit.
6. **Negation and contradiction checks.** NevIR won in replay; FEVER ECE won 12/12.
   - Untested: "Detect contradictions between records or claims" and knowledge-graph edge verification.
7. **Very large label sets.** Haiku's grammar limit failed 993 of 993 attempts at 77 options, where Jev won. At 150 options Jev reached 90.2%. Flat Choice beat the hierarchical version.
   - Untested: catalog normalization or taxonomies with hundreds of labels (E-commerce).
8. **Real-time control loops.** p50 is 127–162 ms, and the hook adds no tool-path latency.
   - Only tested at N≤10 (drone, neo4jev, ultrafast; mario not run). Nothing yet at N≥100 with an outcome label.
9. **Live checking of generated claims.** Calibrated support detection holds on gold evidence.
   - Never tested on LLM-generated text. The claim-check tool passed on README claims (AUC 0.953) but failed on close reasons.
